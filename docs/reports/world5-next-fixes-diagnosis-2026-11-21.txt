Diagnosis after the roof-rule reruns - 21 November 2026

Scope: read-only replay of logs/run_2026-11-21_11-42-51_subscription.jsonl,
the saved journal evidence, and the current code. No new player calls or games.

Finding 1: the player overlooks legal actions
============================================
Decision 108 in attempt 2 is a useful regression case. Its input has 275 sun,
an empty flower pot at row 0 col 0, a 110-HP zombie at x=70 in row 0, and no
cleaner in that row. Wall-nut, Tall-nut and Cabbage-pult are ready. The player
says no cell between the zombie and the house can be planted in time, and
returns keep/actions=[]. The existing validator returns ok for each of those
three plants at (0,0) on that exact recorded state.

Decision 107 has the same legal options at (0,0), but the player requests a
Tall-nut at (0,1). Its snapshot still has a pot there, but that pot disappears
during the 10.18-second call and the order is rejected. Decision 108 then takes
11.39 seconds before returning no action. These observations do not prove that
a different order would have won; they do disprove the idea that all useful
legal options were absent from the observation.

Another example: decision 84 says a cabbage at (0,4) will hit the conehead at
col 1. The board summary correctly reports zero existing attackers in firing
direction. It nevertheless requests the new cabbage farther toward the street.
The input already contains the forward-firing rule. More repetitions of that
rule alone will not establish that the player can apply it reliably.

Recommended interface repair:
- Make plant name, row and column required for plant actions; restrict names to
  actual packet names, eliminating missing-seed and slot-number requests.
- Supply a compact, unranked list/mask of legal target cells per packet, with
  affordability and recharge separate. These are mechanical placement facts,
  not a strategy or an automatically selected move.
- Label observation age, include time-stamped action outcomes and clear support
  availability. Keep live validation because a legal observed target can vanish.
- Replay decisions 84, 107 and 108 offline before committing to another full run.
  Check whether the player can recognize legal placements and firing direction.

Finding 2: the feedback confuses replacement value with cash spent
================================================================
Every attempt starts with 20 free flower pots. In attempt 3 the player buys
20 more pots for 500 sun. The recorder counts 35 lost pots and reports their
replacement value as 875 sun. That replacement-value calculation is arithmetically
correct. It is not 875 sun spent by the player: at least 15 of the destroyed pots
were supplied free, because it only bought 20 altogether.

The reflection interprets pot loss as the dominant economic drain and turns it
into "Put a wall_nut or tall_nut in front of every flower_pot". The player then
spends many turns preparing/protecting pots, while its attacker purchases lag.
Actual attempt-3 packet spending was pots 500, cabbages 800, sunflowers 300,
wall-nuts 250. Pot disappearance alone also does not measure defensive time bought
or damage contributed by the supported plant.

Recommended measurement repair:
- Record initial/free plants separately from player purchases.
- Report actual packet spending, replacement value of lost plants, and purchased
  assets destroyed as distinct quantities. Do not call all of them sun lost.
- Separate initial pots from paid replacement pots in timelines.
- Count an unsupported cause of disappearance as disappearance, rather than
  automatically asserting it was eaten, when removal cause is not known.

Finding 3: the journal overgeneralizes and loses successful behavior
==================================================================
Attempt 2 reaches wave 20 with cabbage-pult, squash, cherry_bomb, wall_nut and
tall_nut. It places 14 cabbages, 9 squashes, 4 cherry bombs and 5 tall-nuts.
Attempt 3 replaces Squash with Peashooter in its loadout, uses no Peashooters,
and never orders a Tall-nut or Cherry Bomb. It reaches wave 12. This is not a
controlled test of any one change, but it shows it did not preserve and refine
the near-winning behavior.

The journal already carries a next_attempt experiment into later prompts.
Therefore adding another experiment sentence is not the missing feature.
Its prose experiment repeatedly insists on a pole-vaulter-lane col-2 Tall-nut
before wave 2. The lane may not be known yet; that exact condition was not
tested. A specific proposed placement is being promoted to an enduring rule.
Roof-specific pot advice also leaks into the general lesson list.

Recommended learning repair:
- Preserve concise factual summaries/loadouts of prior same-level attempts in
  selection and planning, not just in the reflection after the next loss.
- Keep hypotheses explicitly distinct from verified game mechanics.
- Let the player define one measurable experiment per attempt. Carry its goals
  and observed completion state alongside the board so it can notice its own
  omissions; do not have the operator choose or automatically execute the goal.
- Track whether a plan was tested before changing multiple loadout/placement
  choices. Retain the strongest factual result when rewriting lessons.

Finding 4: latency aggravates brittle decisions
==============================================
Median decision latency is 4.39s, 5.18s and 4.62s; maxima 11.81s, 12.88s and
11.07s. In the best attempt, its last two decisions take 10.18s and 11.39s.
An emergency response based on a nearly eaten pot can therefore become illegal
before it is received. The static JSON schema allows malformed actions, and
the player sometimes explains a move without emitting its matching action.

There are zero recorded failed clicks and 150 completed plant actions in these
reruns. The evidence points primarily to decision quality, timing and learning,
rather than repeated focus/click failures. The controller's OK check can also
accept seed recharge as success, so it is not a complete proof of correct target
placement. Future replay instrumentation can cross-check observed target cells.

Recommended sequence
====================
1. Correct the financial/free-pot reporting and action contract.
2. Add compact legal-target facts and test the recorded emergency boards offline.
3. Ground plan/experiment tracking and preserve prior attempt comparisons.
4. Only then run a bounded live comparison with the same initial game conditions
   and journal snapshot. If legal, factually complete replay prompts still yield
   contradictions, measure player reasoning/settings as a separate variable.

These changes improve what the AI can observe and how it learns; they do not
hard-code a roof layout or choose defensive plants for it. Winning remains the
player's responsibility. A performance improvement is a testable hypothesis,
not something established by the current unit tests.
