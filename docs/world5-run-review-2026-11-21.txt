Main cause: ineffective roof damage and incomplete coverage
==========================================================
The first two 5-2 loadouts omitted cabbage_pult despite its availability.
Their actual plantings included seven and five peashooters respectively, with
no lobbers. These peashooters were placed on the sloping back of the roof,
where straight shots hit the roof before reaching distant zombies.

The snapshots support this directly. During attempt 1, decision 94 shows a
peashooter at (1,2) and its approaching zombie at x=522 with 270 HP. Decision 95
still shows 270 HP at x=412. Damage appears only much closer. In row 3, the
zombie remains at 270 HP through x=354 (decision 99); at x=251 it has 150 HP,
then passes the shooter and survives the rest of the lane. Rebuilding a shooter
to the right of a zombie cannot stop that zombie once it has passed.

Attempt 3 brought cabbage_pult and lasted substantially longer, but its first
cabbage was planted about 116 seconds after level_start. Its four cabbages
went to rows 2, 0, 2, and 4. The losing lane, row 1, never received one. Its
defense remained peashooters, and a conehead ate through the row. The final
observed board had zero row-1 attackers and 350 sun, with all packets ready.
This improvement is consistent with better roof damage helping, but the
changed loadout and decisions mean it is not a controlled comparison.

Information defects contributing to those choices
=================================================
1. Plant selection lacks an important rule supplied later during play.
   PICK_SYSTEM and pick_prompt in brains.py explain the roof's pot requirement
   but omit its slope/straight-projectile rule. The decision board does state
   that rule, but by then a player that omitted lobbers cannot change loadout.

2. The row summary can overstate effective damage.
   brains.can_hit_col checks forward column ordering, with special cases for
   multi-directional plants. It does not account for roof slope, shot height,
   or obstacles. _row_summary nevertheless calls eligible plants "can hit the
   closest zombie". This invites treating a back-column peashooter as adequate
   coverage while its peas are actually blocked. The row summary itself defines
   this as a column-order count, but that definition is weaker than the label.

3. Placement facts are less complete in the player's plant descriptions than
   in validation. Potato Mine is described as "Land only", but validation
   rejects it on a Flower Pot. Four 5-2 orders were rejected for this rule,
   including three in attempt 3. The player repeatedly reintroduced the same
   illegal idea despite receiving feedback. Two attempts also spent a loadout
   slot on this unavailable-on-pots option.

4. Mower history is incorrectly inferred.
   The roof boards start with no cleaners. SYSTEM_PROMPT says every row has a
   mower, and journal.LevelRecorder.ending calls an absent mower "already been
   used" even when no firing event was observed. All three 5-2 reflection
   reports make that unsupported claim. This can contaminate lessons about
   spending emergency defenses; absence and observed use must be distinct.

Execution and learning problems
===============================
Eight 5-2 orders targeted missing pots, four requested Potato Mine on a pot,
two tried to add a pot where one already existed, and one used seed "6"
instead of a plant name. These account for all 15 rejected orders.

The board can change during a player call. Median decision time was 4.34s,
4.50s, and 4.60s for the three 5-2 attempts; maxima were 10.19s, 8.17s, and
10.74s. Some missing-pot rejections can therefore reflect stale observations,
not merely misreading the board. In one concrete attempt-2 error, the player
said it would pot and squash the same cell, but actually potted (2,0) and
requested squash at (2,1), where no pot remained.

In attempt 3, the player planted 12 sunflowers and lost five, while the losing
lane remained under-defended. Tall-nut was selected but both orders were
rejected for missing pots. The player also sometimes sent actions inconsistent
with its explanation: decision 151 says it will cancel a sunflower sequence
and build a Tall-nut, but the actual JSON retains the sunflower sequence.
The executor follows the structured actions, not the prose.

The exact queue-stalled-with-unspent-sun measurements are 44.62s, 65.42s,
and 17.44s. These are not all avoidable idle time: they include waiting for
a 100-sun shooter while holding less than 100 sun, and waiting for recharge.
It would be misleading to blame those totals alone on poor economy.

The journal did not converge on the main roof damage problem. Earlier
reflections emphasized adding more shooters and placing them farther back,
while omitting the slope problem. Attempt 2's back-column peashooters then
performed worse. Treat the player's reflection as a hypothesis; its wording
about why it lost is not independent proof of the cause.

Recommended repairs before a fresh run
======================================
Give the same complete terrain/placement facts at selection, play and
reflection; make the row summary describe only verified reach; distinguish
absent cleaners from observed fired cleaners; explicitly specify plant names
in the action contract. These are mechanics/interface corrections and need
not prescribe a winning strategy. Evaluate subsequent player learning after
those factual inconsistencies are removed.
