Roof observation change and three reruns - 21 November 2026

Implemented
===========
Added the same factual roof explanation to plant selection, gameplay and boss
roof observations: straight projectiles fired from the sloped back of the roof
can strike the roof before reaching distant zombies; a peashooter there cannot
cover the whole row, while lobbed projectiles clear the slope. The player still
chooses its own loadout, positions and strategy.

Changed row summaries from "can hit the closest zombie" to a firing-direction
count, explicitly stating that range, slope and obstructions can prevent hits.
Renamed the internal helper to in_firing_direction to match its actual check.
Did not introduce an unverified exact roof range or automatically choose plants.

Added --max-attempts N so wins and losses both consume the requested attempt
budget, with advancement after wins. This was needed because --retries alone
sets a loss limit per level rather than a total attempt limit. Added tests for
mixed wins/retries and for three losses without a fourth navigation.

Validation: 94 tests passed across test_learning, test_campaign and
test_execution. Verified the CLI help. The initial test run exposed an incomplete
synthetic zombie fixture, which was corrected before the passing run.

Did the player understand the new fact?
======================================
Yes, after the first rerun. Cabbage-pult was offered on attempt 1 but not picked.
I verified that the live decision prompt contained the new roof explanation.
On attempt 2 the player chose cabbage-pult as its main damage source, and its
written plan explicitly said to avoid peashooters on the slope because peas hit
the roof. It placed 14 cabbages and reached the final wave.

Attempt 3 again chose cabbage-pult for its roof damage. It also selected
peashooter, but planted none; all eight sustained attackers placed were cabbages.
This is evidence that the factual explanation reached the player's choices.
It is not evidence that the player has learned a stable winning strategy.

Important comparison limit
==========================
All three new attempts began with five roof cleaners. The earlier three 5-2
attempts began with none. Cleaner firing events occurred in the new logs and
remaining cleaners are visible in the second Game Over screenshot. The operator
did not buy cleaners or modify the game state. This changed starting condition,
the continuing journal and different player choices mean the increased wave
reached cannot be attributed solely to the prompt change.

What still failed
=================
The player frequently bought empty pots or extra sunflowers instead of funding
the next sustained attacker, then rebuilt only after zombies reached the plants.
Some cabbages were placed to the street side of a zombie that had already passed
them. The existing factual feedback reported those forward-firing limitations.

Attempt 2 lost row 0 in the final wave. The player had used 14 cabbages, nine
wall-nuts, five tall-nuts, nine squashes and four cherry bombs, but repeated losses
and late rebuilds left that lane without an attacker. Its reflection's final
board reported 375 sun; the last decision snapshot had 275 sun. The final board
also had two cleaners remaining in other rows, which cannot save row 0.

Attempt 3 built eight cabbages, five wall-nuts, six sunflowers and 20 pots. Its
planned tall-nut and cherry bomb were never ordered. It lost all six sunflowers
and six cabbages; the losing row 2 had no attacker and had spent its cleaner in
wave 4. It repeatedly spent 25 sun on another pot while delaying a 100-sun
attacker. Two plant orders omitted the seed name and were rejected.

Rejected orders also involved missing pots, occupied cells, and numerical slot
references. All actually executed clicks registered; these are not focus failures.

The Tall-nut reflection needs a precise reading: its detailed observation says
the planned col-2 placement before wave 2 was not ordered, while Tall-nuts were
placed at other columns. That is supported by the action history. An earlier
operator reading conflated skipping that exact plan with placing no Tall-nuts;
the five completed Tall-nuts do not disprove the narrower plan-adherence claim.
The resulting insistence on the same untested early placement is still a
strategy hypothesis, rather than an established explanation of the loss.
