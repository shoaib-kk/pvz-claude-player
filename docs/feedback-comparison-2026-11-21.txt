New behavior
============
Feedback is timestamped at observation and sent until a decision is accepted.
A request captures an event watermark; acknowledging that reply cannot swallow
events arriving while the player thinks. Player errors or rejected queue edits
do not acknowledge their input. Batches are bounded to eight items, with a count
of omitted older new events. The full current board and queue remain in every
request, including after a player-process reset. The legacy recent list remains
available for inspection but is no longer resent as live decision feedback.

Row summaries directly state when the closest zombie has passed forward-only
shooters. Backward/multidirectional shooters are excluded from that statement.
This describes relative position, not guaranteed projectile reach, damage,
or which move should be chosen. No automatic defensive move was added.

Replay support
==============
replay_decisions.py --compare-to COMMIT loads the formatter from that Git
checkpoint without checking it out or replacing workspace files. Current and
baseline prompts use the same recorded board, queue, loadout knowledge and
historical journal context. Future reflections are excluded.

Old feedback ages are reconstructed from logged event times and estimated
request times, so they carry a ~ marker. New runs log the captured feedback
batch and watermark, allowing exact reuse of what was shown. A replay regression
also covers pending queues: parsing a queue must not overwrite its parent
decision record. This bug was found while expanding the comparison to all boards.

Interpretation
==============
Both versions produced legal placements in both paired cases. There is no
demonstrated playing-strength or win-rate advantage from these two samples.
The wider replay shows a modest reduction in board-text size, while unit tests
verify that old feedback is not repeated after acceptance and in-flight events
are preserved. Placement legality does not simulate damage or prove a save.
